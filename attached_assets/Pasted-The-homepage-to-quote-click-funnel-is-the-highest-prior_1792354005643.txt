The homepage-to-quote click funnel is the highest priority. Make it immediately obvious how visitors can start a quote.

Make data entry extremely simple, with one clear question per screen, large selectable options, minimal typing, and sensible conditional logic.

Include a persistent, animated completion bar throughout the questionnaire.

Collect and persist customer leads in a real database, including incomplete applications.

After the questionnaire, display realistic-looking SAMPLE insurance prices and comparison cards.

Architect the pricing functionality so we can replace the mock pricing adapter with the official QuinStreet QRP integration later without rebuilding the customer-facing funnel.

Do not implement actual carrier integrations yet. Do not represent sample prices as real insurance quotes.

First inspect the current codebase and database configuration. Reuse the existing framework, design system, and database wherever practical. Do not rebuild unrelated parts of the website.

PART 1 — HOMEPAGE CONVERSION FUNNEL

The homepage is critical to conversion.

Review the existing homepage and improve the quote-entry experience without changing the established Uplift branding.

Make the primary CTA highly visible above the fold.

Use an obvious action such as "Get My Quotes" or "Compare Rates."

Ensure the CTA works on desktop and mobile.

Link all relevant existing quote CTAs to the same funnel.

Start with a ZIP code entry screen.

Validate five-digit US ZIP codes.

After the visitor enters a ZIP code and clicks Continue, immediately begin the questionnaire.

Avoid forcing account creation or email entry before the questionnaire.

Avoid unnecessary marketing pages or intermediate screens.

Preserve campaign and UTM parameters through the funnel.

The experience should feel like a natural extension of the existing homepage, not a separate application.

PART 2 — QUESTIONNAIRE UX

Create a sequential questionnaire with one primary question per screen wherever possible.

Display:

Uplift branding.

A horizontal completion bar.

Percentage complete.

Current step or stage.

One clear primary Continue button.

A Back button.

Appropriate loading and validation states.

Use clickable answer cards, segmented controls, radio buttons, date pickers, and searchable dropdowns. Minimize typing.

Save answers as the user progresses. Going backward must not erase previously entered answers.

The completion bar must reflect completed, applicable questions. Update it when the customer adds vehicles or drivers or encounters conditional questions.

Questionnaire sequence

A. Initial questions

ZIP code.

Are you currently insured? Yes / No.

When do you plan to purchase a new policy? Today / Within 7 days / Within 30 days / Later / Just comparing.

Are you a homeowner? Yes / No.

Would you like a home insurance quote? Yes / No.

Explain that bundling may qualify the customer for savings, but never guarantee a discount.

B. Vehicle information

Ask how many cars they want to insure, then collect for each vehicle:

Model year.

Make.

Model.

Trim.

Main use: Personal / Commuting / Business / Rideshare or delivery / Other.

Average daily mileage.

Ownership: Own outright / Financing / Leasing.

Coverage preference: Full coverage / Liability-focused / Help me decide.

Explain full coverage briefly, including collision, comprehensive, and deductibles.

Support multiple vehicles, editing, and removal. Use dependent vehicle dropdowns where practical and provide "I don't know" fallbacks.

C. Driver information

For the primary driver, collect:

Date of birth.

Gender, only where appropriate for the applicable market and rating rules; include a "Prefer not to say" option.

Active driver's license status.

Age first licensed.

Estimated credit score range, including "I don't know" and "Prefer not to say."

Highest education level, only where relevant and legally permitted.

Whether the applicant or an eligible family member served in the military.

Potential discounts: employed, married, student, AAA membership, paperless billing, autopay, driver training, and willingness to participate in an eligible driving-data or telematics program.

Allow multiple discount selections. Explain that selections are not confirmed discounts until eligibility is verified.

D. Driving history

If the applicant IS currently insured, ask:

Number of speeding tickets in the last three years.

Number of insurance claims in the last three years.

DUI/DWI in the last three years.

If the applicant is NOT currently insured, ask first:

Why they do not currently have insurance.

How long it has been since they were last insured.

Then ask the same three driving-history questions.

Use selectable answers, including "Not sure" where appropriate. Display only applicable questions.

E. Overnight parking

Ask where the vehicle is usually parked overnight:

Private garage.

Private driveway.

Carport.

Street.

Apartment or condominium parking lot.

Covered public garage.

Other.

Include an expandable "Why do we ask?" explanation stating that parking location may affect theft and damage risk assessments and that the impact varies by insurer.

F. Additional drivers and contact information

Collect:

First name.

Last name.

Whether the customer wants to add another driver.

Relevant information for each additional driver, including date of birth, license status, relationship, and driving history.

Email address.

Phone number.

Make adding another driver optional, with an obvious "Add another driver" action.

Explain that the email is used to send the customer's quote information. Provide appropriate privacy and contact consent language. Keep marketing consent separate and optional.

Do not request SSN, full driver's license numbers, or payment card details in the demo.

G. Review

Show a concise summary of the customer's answers, vehicles, drivers, and coverage preferences.

Let the customer edit individual sections without restarting the funnel.

When all required questions are complete, show 100% progress and a CTA such as "See My Quote Options."

PART 3 — DATABASE AND LEAD TRACKING

Use the existing database if configured. Otherwise, use Supabase PostgreSQL or another suitable persistent database compatible with the existing stack.

Create persistent records for:

Leads.

Vehicles associated with each lead.

Drivers associated with each lead.

Lead activity events.

Capture:

Unique lead ID and anonymous session ID.

Creation and last-activity timestamps.

Current step and progress percentage.

Lead status: Started, In Progress, Completed, Contacted, Quoted, Converted, or Abandoned.

Answers collected so far.

Vehicle and driver records.

Contact details once provided.

Home quote interest.

Campaign, UTM parameters, and landing-page attribution.

Mock quote request ID and quote results when generated.

Funnel completion timestamp.

Save progress after each step. Retain partial leads even if the user never submits the final form.

Allow returning visitors to resume an authorized saved session where supported.

Implement server-side validation, database access controls, and appropriate retention policies. Never expose database service-role credentials in the browser.

Do not store unnecessary sensitive information. Do not put personal data in analytics event metadata.

PART 4 — MOCK QUOTE ENGINE WITH A FUTURE QRP ADAPTER

Implement a modular pricing interface.

Create a clearly separated abstraction similar to:

QuoteProvider interface.

MockQuoteProvider implementation.

Future QuinStreetQRPProvider implementation.

Use the existing language and architecture. Do not invent QRP API endpoints or payload formats.

The questionnaire must not directly contain pricing logic. It should submit a normalized quote request to the provider abstraction.

For now:

The mock provider returns deterministic, illustrative results based on the submitted answers.

Use fictional insurance companies or clearly labeled sample carriers.

Never suggest Progressive, GEICO, Direct Auto, or another real insurer actually supplied a price.

Store generated mock results and associate them with the lead.

Allow the mock provider to be disabled through configuration when the real integration is introduced.

Keep the result schema flexible enough to map real carrier names, premiums, coverage, deductibles, eligibility, and purchase URLs from QRP later.

Add a clear disclaimer that these are illustrative demonstration estimates, not actual insurance quotes or guaranteed premiums.

The page should have a heading such as "Top options for you."

At the top, display:

Selected coverage summary with an Edit action.

A payment display selector, such as "Due today," "Monthly," or "Six-month premium," where the mock data supports those values.

A clear disclaimer that all prices are illustrative.

Create vertically stacked comparison cards with:

Fictional carrier name and logo placeholder.

Sample premium.

Payment frequency.

A "View" button.

Overall coverage summary.

Expandable "Details" section.

Bodily injury liability limits.

Property damage liability limits.

Comprehensive coverage.

Collision coverage.

Deductible.

Policy term.

Additional relevant coverage where applicable.

Use realistic-looking formatting, whitespace, rounded corners, subtle shadows, and readable typography.

Each card should have a consistent structure, with the price and primary CTA prominently displayed. Cards should be responsive and easy to scan.

Include:

A sort control for lowest illustrative price.

A coverage edit action.

An expandable details section for each result.

An optional side-by-side comparison.

A way to return to the questionnaire without losing answers.

A "Continue" action for each option.

Include an optional rate-monitoring banner only if its functionality is implemented honestly. Do not claim to monitor real rates or send alerts unless that capability actually exists.

Do not fabricate customer reviews, carrier ratings, real carrier offers, or discounts.

PART 6 — SELECTED OPTION AND SIMULATED HANDOFF

When the customer clicks "View" or selects a result:

Open a detailed coverage summary.

Show the selected sample premium and payment assumptions.

Allow the customer to return to the comparison results.

Provide a clearly labeled prototype continuation step.

Do not collect real payment details, issue a policy, claim the customer is insured, or represent the selection as an actual binding transaction.

Keep this transition modular so the future QRP/carrier workflow can replace the mock continuation.

PART 7 — ADMIN DASHBOARD

Create a protected /admin/leads dashboard.

Administrators should be able to:

View all leads, including incomplete ones.

Search by name, email, and phone.

Filter by date, status, campaign, purchase timeframe, and progress.

Open a lead and inspect its answers, vehicles, drivers, and timeline.

See where the applicant stopped.

Review generated mock results.

Update lead status and add internal follow-up notes.

Export authorized lead data to CSV.

View aggregate funnel conversion and abandonment metrics.

Require real authentication and authorization. Do not hardcode admin credentials or expose private customer information publicly.

PART 8 — FUTURE QUINNSTREET QRP INTEGRATION

We intend to connect the application to QuinStreet QRP for actual comparative rating and quote-to-bind.

Prepare the architecture now, but do not pretend the integration is live.

When official documentation and credentials become available:

Map the Uplift questionnaire fields to the documented QRP schema.

Handle missing fields and carrier-specific follow-up questions.

Route sensitive underwriting data through the approved secure QRP/carrier workflow.

Preserve lead attribution and session continuity where supported.

Replace the mock provider with the actual provider implementation.

Display real results only after confirmed responses from the integrated service.

Handle errors, ineligible applicants, and unavailable carriers gracefully.

Do not assume every carrier accepts identical fields or that QRP automatically collects SSN and driver's license data in every integration mode.

Test the complete journey:

Homepage CTA opens the ZIP code entry.

Applicant completes the questionnaire.

Progress updates correctly.

Answers persist when navigating backward.

Multiple vehicles and drivers work.

Conditional insurance-history questions work.

Partial leads are saved.

Completed leads are saved with attribution.

Mock results appear with the correct disclaimer.

Payment-frequency controls update displayed sample amounts correctly.

Results expand to show coverage details.

Selecting an option opens the simulated continuation.

Admins can find and inspect the lead.

Unauthorized visitors cannot access other leads or the admin dashboard.
